# 2026-11-05 — Telephone exchange scaling

Status: published and verified on the production site on 2026-11-05 KST.

- Intended Korean publication date: 2026-11-05.

- Reader question: as the number of users grows, how do all-pairs direct links and a central exchange scale, and what must be managed in place of the removed direct lines?
- Original contribution: a fully enumerated six-endpoint example, the general `n(n-1)/2` calculation, checks at the historically recorded 21/50/64 subscriber counts, a separation of access-line count from concurrent-call capacity, and a five-question checklist for reading shared connection systems.
- Difference from nearby essays: `shannon` studies representation and reliable transmission after a channel exists. `feedback` studies measured output returning to a controller, and `programmable-weaving` studies instructions recorded in cards. This essay instead studies topology, switching state, addressing and capacity when many endpoints share an exchange.

## Sources actually opened on 2026-11-05

1. U.S. Census Office, *Telephones and Telegraphs* (1902): https://www2.census.gov/prod2/decennial/documents/13472615ch1.pdf. Checked the report's distinction between the May 1877 Boston exchange-plan attempt and the January 1878 regularly equipped commercial exchange in New Haven.
2. National Park Service, *Site of the First Telephone Exchange*: https://home.nps.gov/subjects/nationalhistoriclandmarks/site-of-the-first-telephone-exchange.htm. Checked January 28, 1878; 21 initial subscribers; 50 listings by February 21; nominal capacity for 64 customers; two simultaneous conversations; and six connection operations per call.
3. John J. Carty, U.S. patent 353,350, *Switching apparatus and test-circuit for telephone-exchanges*: https://patents.google.com/patent/US353350A/en. Checked the multiple-switchboard description, representation of subscriber lines across boards and the busy-line test problem.
4. Almon B. Strowger, U.S. patent 447,918, *Automatic telephone-exchange*: https://patents.google.com/patent/US447918A/en. Checked the stated goal of subscriber-directed connection without a central operator, the retained central mechanism and control lines, and the reset-after-wrong-selection behavior.
5. Library of Congress, *Historical Telephone Directories and Advertising*: https://blogs.loc.gov/inside_adams/2020/08/telephone-directory-advertising/. Checked the 1878 New Haven first-directory context and the absence of telephone numbers from that directory.

## Independent numerical checks

- Six-endpoint enumeration: `5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15 = 6×5/2` all-pairs links; a central-star access model has six subscriber links.
- At 21 endpoints: `21×20/2 = 210`; `(210-21)/210 = 0.9`, a 90% reduction in link count in the simplified model.
- Theoretical maximum disjoint pairs at 21 endpoints: `floor(21/2) = 10`; this is deliberately contrasted with NPS's record of two simultaneous calls for the specific early board.
- At 50 endpoints: `50×49/2 = 1,225`; reduction against 50 central access lines is `1 - 2/49 = 95.918367...%`.
- At 64 endpoints: `64×63/2 = 2,016`; reduction against 64 central access lines is `1 - 2/63 = 96.825396...%`; the all-pairs/access-line ratio is `2,016/64 = 31.5`.
- These figures compare counts of abstract links, not route length, construction cost, internal switch wiring or historical network expenditure.

## Asset and claim limits

- The SVG is an original Biz2Lab topology diagram. It enumerates 15 direct edges and six central access edges; it does not reproduce a historical switchboard, patent drawing or measured network.
- The historical 21/50/64 counts are not presented as samples of one unchanged operating state. They mark the opening subscriber count, first-directory listings and recorded board capacity.
- The article does not claim that a central exchange removes all wiring or that modern packet networks are identical to early telephony.
- The patent-to-modern-terminology comparison is explicitly labeled as interpretation, not language used in the nineteenth-century documents.
- No first-hand telephone experiment, operator interview, reader response, traffic outcome or AdSense effect is claimed.
